## Short answer

Costa Rica's Executive Decree 43619 requires health insurance for digital nomads with at least USD 50,000 in medical coverage, and the policy must cover the entire authorized legal stay period (Source: `CR_DECREE_43619_2026`, locator: Article 9; verified 2026-01-12).

Both the minimum amount and the full-stay duration must be explicit in the policy evidence. If either is missing, the checker cannot confirm compliance and will return UNKNOWN for that requirement.

## How we evaluate

The checker looks for explicit evidence of the minimum coverage amount and coverage duration. If product documents do not state USD 50,000 or do not show coverage for the full authorized stay, the result is UNKNOWN rather than inferred. See /methodology/ for evaluation logic and the UNKNOWN > Wrong principle.

This route combines two strict requirements: a minimum amount and a full-stay duration. The checker needs both spelled out in evidence. If the certificate lists a coverage amount but no dates, or dates but no amount, the result is still UNKNOWN because one requirement remains unverified.

## Proof package checklist

- Policy certificate showing minimum medical coverage of USD 50,000.
- Policy dates or wording that clearly show coverage for the full authorized legal stay.
- If coverage amounts or dates are on a separate endorsement, include that page.
- Keep a copy of the policy wording that shows the medical coverage amount in case the authority requests clarification.

## Common rejection traps

- Submitting a policy that omits the USD 50,000 minimum coverage amount.
- Coverage dates that do not match the full authorized stay period.
- INFERENCE: Policies that describe coverage generally but omit the minimum amount or duration can lead to UNKNOWN results.
- INFERENCE: Assuming a policy valid for one year automatically matches the authorized stay without checking the visa validity dates.
