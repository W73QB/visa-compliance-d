## Short answer

For the Greece Digital Nomad Visa (National Type D visa), insurance is a required document. The official Work From Greece Digital Nomad Visa checklist (the document the Hellenic Ministry of Foreign Affairs designated portal publishes) requires "Travel insurance, with a period of validity equal to the visa issued, as a minimum, which covers the costs that may arise in the event of repatriation for medical reasons, for emergency medical care and/or emergency hospital care" (Source: `GR_WFG_CHECKLIST_2026`, General Supporting Documents; verified 2026-06-08). So the checker models two rules — insurance is mandatory, and the policy must cover the full visa period — and leaves coverage amount and insurer authorization as UNKNOWN because the checklist states neither.

## What the authority requires

- Insurance is a required document, and it must be valid for a period equal to the visa, covering emergency medical care, hospital care and repatriation. (Source: `GR_WFG_CHECKLIST_2026`, General Supporting Documents; verified 2026-06-08)
- The official checklist accepts travel insurance and states no coverage amount, no insurer-authorization rule, and no deductible restriction. Those attributes are treated as UNKNOWN. The 30,000 EUR figure quoted elsewhere is the separate Schengen short-stay rule, not this national visa.

## How we evaluate

The checker compares each modeled requirement against product evidence. Two rules are encoded from the official checklist: insurance is mandatory, and the policy validity must equal the visa period. A monthly subscription that can be cancelled mid-stay does not assure coverage for the full period, so it is marked YELLOW; a policy that covers the whole visa period is GREEN. Coverage amount and insurer authorization are not stated, so they stay UNKNOWN rather than inferred. See /methodology/ for the full logic and the UNKNOWN > Wrong principle.

## Proof package checklist

- A policy or certificate whose validity equals the visa period (not a month-to-month subscription that can lapse).
- Documented cover for emergency medical care, hospital care, and repatriation for medical reasons.

## Common rejection traps

- Relying on a monthly subscription that can be cancelled before the visa period ends, instead of a full-period policy.
- Assuming a coverage amount or insurer-authorization rule that the official checklist does not state.
- Submitting documentation that does not clearly evidence insurance among the required items.

## FAQ

**Q: Is insurance required for the Greece Digital Nomad Visa?**
**A:** Yes. The Work From Greece portal that the Hellenic MFA designates lists travel insurance among the required documents (Source: `GR_WORKFROMGREECE_DNV_2026`, locator: Required documents list; verified 2026-06-08).

**Q: Does Greece state a coverage amount or require a Greek-authorized insurer?**
**A:** No. The official checklist states no coverage minimum and no insurer-authorization rule, so those are recorded as UNKNOWN. The 30,000 EUR figure is the separate Schengen short-stay rule, not this national visa.

**Q: Why is a monthly subscription marked YELLOW?**
**A:** The checklist requires a validity period equal to the visa. A month-to-month policy can be cancelled mid-stay, so it does not assure coverage for the full period; a full-period policy is GREEN.
