## Short answer

Spain's Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) requires public or private health insurance contracted with an insurance entity authorized to operate in Spain, valid for one year, covering the risks insured by Spain's public health system with no deductible, no copayment, no waiting period, and no coverage limit. Travel insurance with medical assistance is explicitly not accepted (Source: `ES_NLV_LA_EXTERIORES_2026`, Required documents, item 8; verified 2026-06-08).

## What the authority requires

- Health insurance is mandatory for the visa. (Source: `ES_NLV_LA_EXTERIORES_2026`, item 8; verified 2026-06-08)
- The insurer must be authorized to operate in Spain. (Source: `ES_NLV_LA_EXTERIORES_2026`, item 8; verified 2026-06-08)
- The policy must cover the risks insured by Spain's public health system. (Source: `ES_NLV_LA_EXTERIORES_2026`, item 8; verified 2026-06-08)
- Coverage must carry no deductible, no copayment, no waiting period, and no coverage limit. (Source: `ES_NLV_LA_EXTERIORES_2026`, item 8; verified 2026-06-08)
- Travel insurance with medical assistance coverage is not accepted. (Source: `ES_NLV_LA_EXTERIORES_2026`, item 8 note; verified 2026-06-08)

## How we evaluate

The checker compares each modeled requirement against product evidence. A product is GREEN only when its documented terms satisfy every requirement; a conflict (for example a waiting period, a deductible, or a travel-only policy) produces RED; and absent evidence produces UNKNOWN rather than a guess. See /methodology/ for the full logic and the UNKNOWN > Wrong principle.

## Proof package checklist

- An insurance certificate from an insurer authorized to operate in Spain, valid for one year.
- Policy text that explicitly states no deductible, no copayment, no waiting period, and no coverage limit.
- Confirmation that the cover matches the risks insured by Spain's public health system, not travel-only medical assistance.

## Common rejection traps

- Submitting a travel insurance policy: the consulate states these are not accepted for this route.
- A policy with a waiting period (carencia) before benefits start, which conflicts with the no-waiting-period rule.
- A policy with a deductible or copayment, or with an annual coverage cap.
