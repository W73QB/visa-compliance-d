## Short answer

Italy's short-term self-employment visa is a Schengen (Type C) visa, and the Consulate General of Italy requires proof of medical-expenses coverage of at least 30,000 euro (or 50,000 US dollars) for the duration of the trip. The consulate is explicit that only *medical expenses* coverage counts: trip cancellation, lost baggage, and repatriation of remains do not count toward the minimum (Source: `IT_SELFEMP_SF_INSURANCE_2026`, Travel Medical Insurance notice; verified 2026-06-08).

## What the authority requires

- Medical insurance is mandatory for the visa. (Source: `IT_SELFEMP_SF_INSURANCE_2026`, Travel Medical Insurance notice; verified 2026-06-08)
- Medical-expenses coverage must be at least 30,000 euro (or 50,000 US dollars) for the duration of the trip. (Source: `IT_SELFEMP_SF_INSURANCE_2026`, Travel Medical Insurance notice; verified 2026-06-08)
- Only medical-expenses coverage counts toward the minimum; non-medical benefits do not. (Source: `IT_SELFEMP_SF_INSURANCE_2026`, Travel Medical Insurance notice; verified 2026-06-08)

## Short-term versus long-term: why this route is the short-stay one

The Italian self-employment visa (lavoro autonomo) comes in short-term and long-term forms. This page models the short-term variant, a Schengen Type C visa for stays of up to 90 days, under the Schengen rule quoted above. The long-term (Type D) visa works differently: applicants register with Italy's national health service after arrival, so the consulate's short-stay 30,000 euro figure does not transfer to it. Because the official short-stay notice is the document that states an explicit, checkable coverage amount, the checker models that route and records the long-term terms as UNKNOWN rather than reusing a number where it does not apply.

## Proof package checklist

- A certificate or letter from the insurer stating medical-expenses coverage of at least 30,000 euro, valid for the trip dates.
- Confirmation that the figure is medical-expenses coverage, not a combined travel-benefits cap.
- A policy valid across the Schengen area for the full period of the short stay.

## Common rejection traps

- Counting trip cancellation, baggage, or repatriation-of-remains limits toward the 30,000 euro: the consulate states these do not count.
- A medical-expenses limit below 30,000 euro.
- Submitting an insurance card instead of a formal certificate or letter from the insurer.

## FAQ

**Q: How much medical coverage is required?**
**A:** At least 30,000 euro (or 50,000 US dollars) in medical-expenses coverage for the duration of the trip (Source: `IT_SELFEMP_SF_INSURANCE_2026`; verified 2026-06-08).

**Q: Is travel insurance accepted?**
**A:** Yes. The consulate notice lists travel medical insurers as acceptable, provided the medical-expenses coverage meets the minimum.

**Q: Does repatriation count toward the minimum?**
**A:** No. Non-medical services such as repatriation of remains do not count toward the 30,000 euro requirement.
